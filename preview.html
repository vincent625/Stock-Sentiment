How fast does the market react?

The literature is heterogeneous, but it gives a consistent design implication: highly salient hard news can be incorporated in minutes; broader sentiment effects can remain measurable over tens of minutes to a few hours; residual underreaction or drift can persist for days for some event classes. That is why the final study needs both 1–5 minute data and daily horizons.

Exact x × y search surface

For each ticker i, time t, sentiment lookback x and forward horizon y, estimate the association between a relevance/novelty-weighted sentiment signal and future abnormal return. Intraday x ranges from 5 minutes to one day; daily x extends to 20 trading days. Forward y runs from 1 minute to 20 trading days.

Measurement and statistical design

Signal. For each story/security pair, define article_score = sentiment × relevance × novelty × source_quality. Aggregate only stories published before the as-of timestamp, optionally with exponential decay. Keep news volume separate. Deduplicate syndicated stories and updates, and remove reactive headlines that simply narrate a price move that has already occurred.

Outcome. Use future abnormal log return, not future price level: stock return minus a pre-event beta-adjusted QQQ (or market + sector) return. Intraday response horizons: 1m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 4h, close, +1d. Drift horizons: 1/2/3/5/10/20 trading days.

Primary statistic. Cross-sectional Spearman information coefficient across available names at each timestamp/day, with Pearson as secondary. For overlapping returns use HAC/Newey–West and block bootstrap. Adjust the x×y grid with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR. Select a stable OOS plateau rather than the maximum raw correlation cell.

Reaction speed. Compute SpreadCAR(h): cumulative abnormal return of the top sentiment quintile minus the bottom quintile. Report T50 and T80, the earliest horizons reaching 50% and 80% of a pre-declared terminal response (e.g. 4h or one session). Repeat by positive/negative news, novelty, event class, and regular-hours/after-hours release.

Limitations and robustness

  • Current top-10 bias: fixed 2026 holdings are useful for today's universe but not a point-in-time index backtest.
  • Multiple testing: searching many x/y cells creates false discoveries unless the validation split and FDR correction are pre-specified.
  • Overlapping horizons: 5d/20d forward returns overlap heavily; naive standard errors are too optimistic.
  • News endogeneity: articles can be written because the stock already moved. Exclude price-reactive headlines and use first-publish timestamps/story versions.
  • Aggregation bias: weight stock-event observations carefully so one heavily covered name/day does not dominate the result.
  • Alphabet share classes: GOOG and GOOGL are the same issuer. Collapse to one issuer observation for cross-sectional IC so a single Alphabet story is not counted twice; keep both only for a share-class robustness check.
